The handoff brief for Jira

Baton answers the question someone asks when work lands on their desk: where does this stand, what was decided, what is blocked, what is still open — and who knows the history?

Jira keeps the raw record: comments, field changes, links. Baton reads that record and writes the brief, with every claim quoted from a real comment, its author and its date. No summary is invented, and no model is asked to guess: the analysis is deterministic and every line can be checked against the issue in one click.

Issue panel

Decisions on record, open blockers, unanswered questions, promised next steps, handoff history, contributors and context links — for the issue you have open.

Handoff inbox

Work that changed hands in the last 14 days, with anything quiet for a week or more flagged, because that is where a handoff usually gets dropped.

Handoff risk

A 0–100 score built from explainable signals: open blockers, unanswered questions, no assignee, staleness, repeated handoffs, an overdue date.

Post the brief

Write the brief into the issue as a comment, so the next person finds it inside the work item itself.

Built to stay inside Jira

Baton runs on Atlassian Forge. Every request is made as the person using it, so Jira's own permissions decide what the app can read or write. It has no server of its own, sends nothing outside Atlassian, and stores nothing: the privacy policy is short because there is little to describe.
